# PR190 — Passing Realism, Effective Playing Time & Articulated Contact

## 1. Executive findings and readiness

This is a staged engineering change. **Canonical remains NOT READY.** B
`state-value-v1` remains default; the reserved independent confirmation cohort is
unopened. The improvement in WW reception is real, but does not establish realistic
circulation, attacking creation, effective playing time or policy superiority.

The isolated reception defect was an absolute attribute-derived quality cutoff:
the engine declared a heavy touch before executing a reachable cushioning contact.
The same 4 m/s ball could be stopped by the existing foot solver at skill 40, yet
the reception layer manufactured a loose ball. Reception now executes one finite
surface impulse, including local contact difficulty, and applies that actual result.
A separate universal 350 ms loose-ball contact ban is replaced by each player's
acquisition preparation and motor recovery. A prepared different defender can act.

## 4. Authoritative passing lifecycle

`PassLifecycleDiagnostics` is an external state-transition observer. It records
canonical release/resolution IDs, true passer/receiver profiles, release vectors,
spin, targets, movement, awareness delay, approach trajectory, first contact,
actual contact position, control, surface execution when present and later recovery.
Bounded decision/preparation traces are copied separately; they are not outcomes.
Neither observer uses RNG, grants possession, moves a player or changes the clock.

Trajectories are sampled at 100 ms with canonical resolution contacts retained;
release can be observed at the end of its 25 ms tick, and that precision limit is
flagged. Settled-pass launch still lacks a complete articulated surface receipt;
missing fields are not invented. Existing first-time/reception surface receipts
include the actual foot, impulse, incoming/outgoing velocity and spin.

Reports retain the engine manifest, observer hash, runner hash, input hash and
archive hash. Samples use two lowest seeded hashes per distance/pressure/movement/
cause stratum plus 24 independently seeded samples. The selection rule and IDs
are stored in every match receipt. All pass records remain in compact gzip archives.

## 5. Quantified causes and retained negatives

The PR189 WW/H replay records **988 first-touch failures in 1,559 attempts**.
WW/C records **1,043 in 1,600**; WW/A records **742 in 1,915**. Other important
observed causes include uncontrolled opponent deflections and real lane contacts.
These are development observations, not universal policy properties.

The taxonomy distinguishes target selection, obsolete target, inaccurate launch,
launch velocity, spin/bounce, receiver approach, reaction, failed first touch,
lane interception, landing race, deflection, boundary, statistics, other physical
control and undetermined. An aim error alone is not promoted into a causal finding.
Unsupported distinctions remain `undetermined`; zero observed examples do not
establish that a mechanism is absent.

Early observer versions could reuse an old reception point at a later resolution.
The current observer prefers the current canonical pass contact coordinate. Earlier
raw archives remain unchanged; interpret resolution geometry using their canonical
`diagnostic.actualContactPoint`, not an obsolete reception's coordinate. Observer
versions are hashed separately. Canonical self-reclaims are reported separately
from statistical pass completions; the statistics correctly exclude self-passes.

The first repair was rejected as complete because it allowed overly ideal
cushioning of awkward impacts and changed legacy selection assumptions. Its full
15-match `passing-final` series and earlier 600-second trials remain retained.
`final-v5` and the later uninstrumented continuation series were superseded;
partial runs are not counted as completed development matches. The complete
`measured-final` stage also exposed a later transfer defect: the loose-ball helper
discarded the computed spin/height after a failed reception. The final `spin-final`
stage preserves those physical quantities and reruns all 15 matches on the actual
head runtime manifest `0420f51f42962d103ceee040f90745db83b868708763677bc712d9016f9890e8`.
Earlier receipts are preserved under their original versions; no negative is
replaced with the final result or described as confirmation.

## 6. Canonical corrections

`resolveReceptionOutcome` now uses `executeFootContact` and returns the actual
surface execution. The canonical control transition applies that same impulse
once. A poor first touch leaves the real outgoing velocity and spin; it no longer
replaces the ball with a synthetic 2–4 m/s escape, nor assigns a future waypoint as
the present ball position. Secure control still requires real height/foot reach.

Local first-touch quality limits the connected surface impulse. A prepared cushion
has a bounded 12 cm giving-way stroke within the existing contact envelope, relative
to the model's 24 cm nominal contact path. These are engineering parameters, not
an empirically validated physiological calibration. Failed and partial contacts
remain possible at high speed, awkward presentation, pressure or insufficient preparation.
No global passing-completion coefficient, turnover roll or pass quota was added.

The low airborne reception sweep now excludes the outgoing kicker, consistently
with ground-pass contact detection. Merely remaining inside one's own reach volume
after release is not another incoming reception. Later loose-ball recovery remains legal.

## 7. Reception, interception and second balls

In the team-quality laboratory, unopposed lateral cases at team skill 40 improve
from 13/24 completed to 23/24, including a retained unresolved case rather than a
guaranteed success. Moving-receiver cases improve from 6/24 to 19/24; five unclaimed
cases remain. At skill 80 the lateral and moving-reference results remain 23/24
and 24/24. These pooled distance/angle counts are not a professional-match target.

At the identical reachable 4 m/s contact, all 24 skill-40 second-ball controls now
use the finite cushion rather than an automatic heavy touch. Firm 10/30 m/s,
early/prepared controls retain natural heavy/failed results. The initial independent
passer/receiver contrasts show why changing only the passer attribute could not fix WW.

The global 350 ms ban after a loose ball is removed. Acquisition retains its own
finite preparation, episode validation, real foot contact and seeded contested
control. `motorContactReadyAt` also gates the same player's acquisition after a
touch. An independently prepared defender does not borrow the receiver's recovery.
Interceptions/deflections remain separate from a controlled possession award.

## 8. Passing circulation and tactical value

Bounded support forecasts previously assumed a universal 18 m/s delivery. Their
travel/lead estimates now use the ordinary weighted ground-pass time envelope,
with visible opponent motion and uncertainty. They receive no hidden opponent skill
or future outcome. The legacy evaluator's broader reception-retention calibration
was deliberately retained after a proposed change regressed its decision contracts.

Ordinary controlled-player reception now receives the same anticipation as an NPC
when no important incoming choice exists. Important opportunities and explicit
human intentions retain agency. Exact whole-team statistics and player positions
are checked against the NPC continuation; no hidden first-touch advantage is granted.

Reciprocal circulation and weak chance creation remain unresolved. A completed
pass is not evidence of tactical gain. No backward-pass rule, forward reward,
mandatory carry, shot count or universal post-reception delay is added by this PR.

`selection-and-circulation.json` reconstructs candidates from recorded bounded
scenes, separately from canonical release/outcome. For WW/H, the mean change from
decision target to released target is 0.423 → 0.136 m; completed-pass travel error
is 1.788 → 0.409 s. The completed samples differ (19 versus 912 receptions), so
this is not a paired estimate of forecast improvement on identical passes. B/R3
use the legacy selector and have no bounded-candidate mapping; their null fields
are missing coverage, not zero error. Last preparation traces can belong to an
older possession; their age is explicitly not continuous preparation duration.

The same audit finds 159 adjacent reciprocal completed pairs in current WW/H,
31 with a near return and no measured extra clearance or clear teammate lane.
SS/B changes from 180 pairs (20 such returns) to 207 (38); SW/B from 90 (14) to 106 (9). The definition is retained in the receipt. These are observations at
release and contact, not proof that every exchange was useless, and they do not
drive an action penalty. Improved reception exposes circulation that the earlier
first-touch defect had prevented.

## 9. Actual ball-in-play time

`measureEffectivePlayingTime` uses **all** `elapsedDeadBallSeconds`, not the
qualifying added-time allowance. Elapsed time partitions into in-play and dead-ball;
regulation plus actually played added time partitions the same canonical duration.
Injury/substitution/discipline/goal/restart reason segments have an explicit exclusive
priority. Evicted ledger detail is reported as unclassified duration, not lost time.
Headless human pause duration is zero; other hosts must supply it or leave it unknown.

WW/A PR189 played 88.405 in-play minutes in 92 elapsed minutes. Its 215.7 dead seconds
include only three throw-ins, twelve foul episodes and one goal episode, alongside
period/substitution preparation. This is principally sparse interruption frequency;
adding an arbitrary wait to every restart would conceal that problem.

Current high effective time remains a readiness blocker. No throw-in, foul or pause
was inserted to match an active-play percentage. Restart travel, legal distances,
period lengths and running-clock semantics remain authoritative.

## 10. Referee-added time and empirical context

[IFAB Law 7](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/the-duration-of-the-match/) was
checked on 2026-10-25: two 45-minute halves, a minimum announced allowance which can
increase, and completion of a terminal penalty remain intact. A non-qualifying
portion of an ordinary restart is still physically dead time.

As external context, the Premier League's
[2024/25 season analysis](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4271974) reports
57.07 in-play minutes at its publication point. Opta's
[2026/27 early-season measurement](https://theanalyst.com/articles/premier-league-rules-throw-ins-goal-kicks-ball-in-play)
reports 56:35 after matchday five. These different cohorts and dates are references,
not a required constant. [UEFA's 2024/25 Champions League statistics](https://www.uefa.com/uefachampionsleague/history/seasons/2025/statistics/)
include leading club passing accuracy around 89–92%, likewise not a target for every
simulated team, tactical style or action difficulty.

## 11. Passing/time interaction configurations

The physical baseline and corrected engine are paired on the same frozen inputs.
Time measurement is observational: baseline with reporting is the same execution
as baseline without a clock intervention; corrected passing with reporting is the
same physical execution as corrected passing alone. There is therefore no separate
claimed clock-mechanism treatment. Fewer total passes are interpreted alongside
in-play minutes, pass rate and shots, rather than automatically declared an improvement.

## 12. Lower-body geometry and limited compliance

`lowerBodyPose` is an explicit experimental, optional state. Old saves and the
frozen ordinary development inputs omit it. The connected pelvis/hip/thigh/knee/
shin/ankle/foot chain uses player height, fixed segment lengths and bounded posture;
inverse kinematics preserves connection. Stance/crouch/hip transitions advance in
the existing physical step. Actual contact foot origins come from that geometry.
Supporting leg, active leg and plant balance are represented.

For explicitly posed pairs, movement clearance depends on orientation, mass scale,
strength and closing speed, with at most 2 cm compliance per body and a retained
solid core. The same integrator constrains the motion; no second physics engine or
coordinate teleport is introduced. The captured restart also passes with poses.
This is a foundation, not validated human tissue/crowd dynamics or a full gait model.

## 13. Defensive surfaces, leg gaps and goalkeeper stance

The connected capsule descriptions are not yet a replacement for every ballistic
body collider. General shin/knee/thigh deflection impulses, a complete deformable
wall, goalkeeper gap closing and true nutmegs are **not implemented**. No success
probability or cosmetic trick is presented as physical geometry. Ordinary matches
retain the legacy body path until the experimental subset is validated further.

[IFAB Law 12](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/fouls-and-misconduct/) was checked
on 2026-10-25. Physical contact is not itself a foul; careless, reckless and excessive
force remain distinct. This PR changes no disciplinary classification or required
restart distance and makes no new push automatically legal.

## 14. Fast versus prepared action

The contact apparatus varies awareness/preparation at identical incoming states,
including firm balls and weak/strong players. Preparation changes finite cushioning
capacity; failed reception retains the actual outgoing ball. This is not the full
requested tactical immediate-pass versus delayed-pass choice experiment. Lane
closure, alternate passing surfaces, toe/backheel execution and deception remain
staged behind ordinary passing/receiving and contact diagnostics.

## 15. Match Lab camera

The slider, preference schema and wheel clamp accept 0–1.5. Minimum zero, default
0.35, reset and the original zoom mapping for 0–1 are preserved. Stored preferences
round-trip through the schema. Projection/picking tests preserve all old touchline
cases and cover a visible central actor at the new maximum across presets/orbit;
close overview intentionally crops distant touchlines. Goal aiming retains its separate camera branch.
There is no camera redesign or change to match stepping.

Live visual QA was attempted: the in-app browser could not reach the local Vite
server and native Edge access timed out at app approval. No screenshot or completed
visual browser check is claimed. Schema, projection, picking, input and reset
regressions are exercised by the automated renderer suites.

## 18. Determinism, performance and compatibility

The complete legacy telemetry contract retains all 80 exported fields, all 2,400
ticks, schemas, identity ledgers and assertions. Both PR189 seeds and current seeds
repeat in eight fresh processes. New hashes are versioned; old payloads are unchanged.
Some old negative fixtures assumed a low skill alone forced a slow heavy touch.
They now provide an explicit difficult surface/firm arrival and let the actual
outgoing ball be chased. Recovery/throw legality/turnover identity assertions remain.

The report distinguishes full observer-neutral repeats from different-workload
performance comparisons. Fixed 40 Hz stepping, 28 attributes, player identities,
policy IDs/default, offside, goalkeeper law, injuries/substitutions and human
finishing agency remain. Additional posture data is optional and schema-validated.

The final 8,000-tick serial performance probe records CPU user+system time of 10.828 s
for baseline, 11.016 s for head, 9.828 s for head with the observer and 9.936 s
for explicit poses; RSS is approximately 350/405/427/444 MB. JIT warmup, serial
ordering, shared-host activity and different physical continuations confound
these timings. They do not establish an observer speedup or precise overhead.
The head/observed final state hashes agree. The complete final WW/A match repeats
225,827 ticks with both lifecycle and match-flow observers disabled, reproducing
the entire canonical final state. Three earlier complete WW matches independently
agree with their uninstrumented physical stage. Broader browser/crowd profiling remains open.

## 19. Remaining blockers and incomplete gates

High passing volume, repeated circulation, poor shot creation and roughly 87–90
in-play minutes in the observed WW examples still falsify general readiness.
Receiver inactivity/unclaimed balls, bounded versus canonical forecasts and
defensive opportunity calibration need further causal work. Not every inaccurate
launch or bounce can yet be independently attributed. Advanced body collision and
timed-release gates remain open as listed above.

The empirical reference is not a hidden target. No score, possession count, pass
counter, random turnover multiplier or stopped-clock correction was used to make
aggregate football look realistic. No independent cohort was opened for tuning.
