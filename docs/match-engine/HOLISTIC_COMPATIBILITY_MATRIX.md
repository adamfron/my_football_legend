## Release ownership: the contact-v1 / pending question

`matchActions` calculates `temporalPass = selectsPendingPass(...)`. It asks the existing
planner for nominal-contact metadata, then its release-time kick branch explicitly requires
**`!temporalPass`**. An eligible temporal low pass calls `commitPendingPass`; the real ball
and executed-pass events stay unchanged at selection. `pendingStrikeInterval` later calls
the finite foot impulse once on actual contact. The compatibility test selects both flags
and asserts pending state, unchanged ball and no release-time `lastKickExecution`.

Thus the two selectors can coexist **without two impulses on the same release**. The
contact-v1 perturbation helper used to prepare the pending plan is not a live ball impulse.
Shots eligible for contact-v1 remain synchronous; pending low-pass support does not turn
shots into temporal strikes. Loft/cross, backheel/outside/turning exclusions, first-time,
headers/volleys, keeper distributions, free kicks and other unsupported releases preserve
their original execution/uncertainty paths. They must be counted as explicit fallbacks.

admits noisy visible positions and derives aged uncertain motion; physical truth does not
become a cognition oracle. PR203/204/205 use related memory and local-reception routes,
but their different handoffs and known regressions prevent treating their labels as an
interchangeable observation contract. PR206 did not validate preparation mediation.

Policy B keeps full-world engineering rankings; O/H/J use bounded candidate interfaces.
Selecting height-aware and observed-loft switches cannot make both consumers act under
the same policy. The manifest identifies inactive consumers, and activation requires an
actual witness. `configured`, `eligible`, `invoked`, `physicallyExecuted`, `outcomeObserved`
and `fullyEvaluated` remain separate stages; unobservable invocation is `null`.
