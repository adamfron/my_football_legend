## Event and information contract

Before each numerical experiment record the registry IDs, immutable source fingerprint, exact initial state/configuration, actor, opposition, seed, policy, optional features, information snapshot and its age, candidate list, selection, request time, contact/release time, observation horizon and censoring rule. State the exact predicted event and its denominator. Distinguish:

- A heuristic utility or uncertainty score from a probability claim.
- The player's bounded subjective estimate from a privileged engineering forecast.
- A generated candidate, a currently admissible candidate, a preparatory intention, a requested action and an actual physical release.
- Accurate delivery, a touch, controlled possession and later retained team possession.
- Implementation correctness, physical feasibility, empirical calibration, tactical validity, full-match transfer and independent confirmation.

PR195's truth forecasts are diagnostics. `evaluatePassDecision` reads actual opponent abilities; its result must never be passed to bounded O/H/J as if observed. `estimateUsefulContact` instead uses a common uncertain opponent envelope. Its truth-only fallback when a defender is unseen is explicitly not player knowledge. The hidden-rating regression verifies that scene, candidates and all three bounded selections remain unchanged.

## Reproducible counterfactual procedure

`captureCalibrationSnapshot(state, actorId)` accepts an actual saved canonical state. It retains that state, the bounded scene, candidates and O/H/J decisions, the canonical shooting menu and all six historical/full-world policy rankings. It does not pretend that the historical selectors are bounded. Information absent from a bounded scene remains absent.

`runCalibrationTrial` clones the input, requests **one** supplied ordinary action through `resolveMatchAction(..., 'autonomous_npc')`, then steps both autonomous teams at 0.025 s. Requests are labelled `forced_experiment`, including a request identical to the policy's choice. No contact, ownership, future movement or successful outcome is granted. A rejected request remains rejected even if a later autonomous action succeeds. The experiment changes no canonical controller.

The pilot additionally advances two fixtures autonomously for two seconds, captures the resulting canonical tick states, and tries up to four available alternatives from each identical state. Selection and hold are prioritised, followed by a pass/shot if the bounded candidate list contains one. In the two recorded captures that list contains only hold/carry alternatives; no captured pass/shot continuation is claimed. The snapshot stores the chosen policy action separately. These are controlled fixture continuations, **not** a representative sample of states harvested from full matches. The separate scene catalogue contains forced pass/shot requests. Broader full-match state capture, including eliminated attacking alternatives, is a follow-up gate.

PR195 stores initial/final hashes, complete result/observation/trace hashes, candidate snapshots, all transition witnesses and 0.25 s spatial samples. The reproduction runner regenerates the full 40 Hz outputs before comparing compact rows. It never regenerates or overwrites an existing receipt in check mode. Inputs and methods are sufficient to reproduce omitted duplicate tick streams.

## PR195 pilot definitions and scope

The frozen [protocol](../performance/pr195/pilot-protocol.json) specifies 18 scenes × seven matchups × two seeds × two mirrored geometries = **504 requested trials**. Each horizon is six seconds, unless a canonical terminal condition ends the phase. Skill matchups: 20:20, 40:40, 60:60, 80:80, 100:100, 40:80 and 80:40. All own-team attributes take the attacking band and opposing attributes the defending band; these are controlled homogeneous bands, not a claim about real squads. Lateral mirroring also swaps the dominant foot.

**Pilot A: PASS-001/003/006/007.** Safe, progressive, through, long, pressured-receiver, moving-defender and wrong-direction-defender scenes. Bad target directly at a defender and an unseen/outdated receiver location are negative controls. The stale-receiver case is an experimentally supplied obsolete target, not a claim that the active-memory controller produced it. Completion is an intended-receiver physical touch before the original delivery's first other control/restart. Receiver control is separate. Retention requires receiver control and own-team ownership one second later. Delivery proximity, defender touch, defender control, loose ball at horizon and exit/restart are reported separately. A six-second timeout without a resolved episode is censored, not a failure manufactured for scoring.

**Pilot B: DEF-001/002/003/004.** The same canonical pass episodes distinguish defender proximity, finite-foot access at a sampled instant, a contact witness, useful disruption (executed contact with velocity change greater than 0.5 m/s), and defender ownership. These flags overlap. A body-flight collision can occur between tick samples; absence of sampled foot access does not prove absence of a canonical body contact. Contact is not necessarily control.

Thirty additional isolated locomotion trials prescribe one fixed 8 m movement objective at five defensive skill bands, with toward/away/transverse initial velocity and both mirrors. They use canonical body integration, turning, plant balance and fitness, and compare actual arrival inside the estimator's 0.8 m radius with both ETA models. The objective is prescribed; no position or arrival is forced. This isolates motion-model mismatch. Arrival at a target during autonomous football is a different measurement: tactical target choice and changing ball trajectory confound a direct ETA-error interpretation. Do not fit an ETA coefficient to that confounded difference.

**Pilot C: SHOT-001/004/006/007.** Good position, poor position with an outlet, poor position with a distant outlet/nearby pressure, difficult incoming ball, blocked shot, keeper away and rebound-capable geometry. These scene names are hypotheses to inspect: an outlet is not certified safe and the trapped player's every continuation is not assumed impossible. A remote high ball is an infeasible-contact control. A safe hold control prevents interpreting every positive result as evidence that attacking is always correct.

For each request retain candidate family and same-intent membership separately, canonical contact feasibility, actual release, all policy values/ranks and the matching requested shot ID's outcome. A later unrelated shot cannot rescue the original request. Goalkeeper action, blocks, frame contacts, subsequent ownership and attacking corners are separate consequences. A possible rebound geometry does not guarantee a rebound or corner. Corner and second-ball horizon flags can include subsequent autonomous football and must not be interpreted as direct probabilities for the initial shot.

The current enum/menu does not implement a complete toe-poke/overhead/bicycle finishing chain. Existing volley and header labels do not prove articulated airborne foot/head contact realism. No substitute bicycle action or named spectacular-goal chance is introduced.

## Scoring, uncertainty and selection bias

`scoreCalibration` reports probability-interval reliability, Brier score, prediction minus observed rate, log loss clipped at epsilon 1e-12, and Wilson 95% intervals. The report includes AUC to distinguish discrimination from calibration, and tables by scene and skill matchup. Exact zero/one forecasts are retained; log-loss clipping is an explicit numerical reporting convention.

Mirrors and paired seeds are dependent observations. Wilson intervals are **descriptive** here; nominal independent-sample coverage is not asserted. There are only four requests per scene/matchup. No real-world calibration or stable rare-event rate follows from this pilot. Larger development programmes should use preregistered clustered/paired uncertainty estimates, independent geometries, unseen opponents, foot/orientation/incoming-state strata, and held-out measurements after freezing the candidate. Do not choose a parameter from these 504 cases and report the same cases as confirmation.

Natural matches supply a policy-selected sample. Forced alternatives address one part of that bias, while the fixed fixture catalogue introduces another. Expand real-state harvesting before making full-match claims. Keep absurd passes, genuine interceptions, safe continuations/holds, infeasible contact, stale information and almost useless shot trajectories. A pipeline that cannot show a justified cautious decision is not adequate for tuning decision policy.

## Future tactical laboratories

### Goalkeeper attacking the last corner — GK-008/009, TACT-010

Prerequisites: explicit competition context for aggregate score, advancement, extra time and relevant goal-difference stakes; a bounded contextual decision; physically available aerial participation; real travel, fitness, ordinary contacts and empty-goal consequences. Current score/time alone is insufficient. The absent competition-advancement context is a blocker, not permission to invent it in a fixture and call it implemented.

Freeze paired variants: one goal to force extra time; one goal to advance; draw sufficient to preserve advantage; a late corner with substantial time remaining; a league game where goal difference matters; low/high goalkeeper aerial ability. Include a case where staying in goal is preferable. Measure physical travel/arrival, contact, subsequent empty-goal counterattack and return cost. No special goalkeeper scoring probability or automatic late-deficit run is allowed.

### Quick versus prepared corner — REST-004–008/012, FIT-008

Begin with the same lawful award, body positions, ball and paired seed. In the prepared branch tall aerial players walk/jog to real positions while fast cover protects the counterattack. In the quick branch execute a legal temporary opening before everyone is ideally organised. Tactical setup completion is not a law. A liveness observer is not a universal deadline.

Measure award-to-ball-ready, legal-ready, tactical-ready and actual release separately; participant travel, workload, available aerial targets, defender organisation, actual contact, second balls and counterattack exposure. For the covering defender measure attacker progress, seconds gained by containment, teammates' recovery, sprint work, later physical duel and restored shape. Successful delay need not win possession.

### Contact, pain, recovery and fair play — CONTACT-001–015

Preserve the existing injury system while auditing a separate contact/fall → temporary reduced participation → physical getting-up/reorientation → restored function or medical assessment lifecycle. No randomly inserted pain episode or universal 30-second pause may be used to raise dead-ball time.

Preregister: brief non-injury knock; player temporarily down while play lawfully continues; serious injury; suspected head injury with explicit referee/medical priority; assessment delaying an existing award; ball already live in a shot/rebound; voluntary sporting kick-out when the incident is visible or unseen; referee already intervening; optional return or refusal after the legal restart. Distinguish convention from a mandatory Law, and preserve human/team choice.

Current rules reviewed on 2026-10-26: [IFAB Law 5, injuries](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/the-referee/), [Law 8, dropped ball](https://theifab.com/laws/latest/the-start-and-restart-of-play/), and [2026/27 changes](https://theifab.com/law-changes/latest/). Slight injury can allow play to continue; serious injury requires referee intervention. Treatment/re-entry has specific exceptions and requirements. Do not reduce suspected concussion to the generic assessment timer; review the applicable [additional permanent concussion substitution protocol](https://www.theifab.com/laws/latest/additional-permanent-concussion-substitutions-protocol/) before implementation. Competition applicability and the intended rules edition must be explicit.
