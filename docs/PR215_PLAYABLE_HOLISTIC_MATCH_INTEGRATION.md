## 4. Admissible clinch geometry

The extracted `PASSIVE_OUTFIELD_BODY` constants preserve the existing sphere exactly:
radius **0.72m**, centre height **0.9m**. The upright torso solid core remains **0.34m** in
radius; the ball remains **0.11m**. These different representations are explicitly identified.

At a grounded ball centre z=0.11m, two such spheres can both touch it only if their planar
centres are at most **0.509116882m** apart. This conflicts with the **0.68m** upright core
separation. Thus the historical ground apparatus cannot become a valid upright low-leg
clinch by adding another player. No tolerance enlargement or knee/ankle reinterpretation
is used. Existing feet lie near grass and cannot be treated as upper-body contact surfaces.

The admissible alternative uses two centres 1m apart and supports the ball on their upper
surfaces at **z=1.562495283m**. Eight 25ms support steps retain the two contacts, with no
ownership, artificial velocity or ejection. A third actual moving passive body reaches the
ball; its nearest torso-core separation stays **0.693108938m**, above 0.68m. Existing CCD and
rebound supply the outgoing impulse; subsequent normal flight escapes and clears/rearms
the contact latches. An incompatible legacy support input remains finite and unresolved
under the existing eight-pass solver. No release timer, fall state or referee rule is added.

This is a source-backed **upper-sphere geometry/support primitive**, not a canonical
high-ball clinch, articulated legs or an additional whole-match readiness pass.
